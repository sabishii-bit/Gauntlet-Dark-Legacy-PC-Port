# 4.0.0-alpha.3

## Added

- Quit on the title screen's main menu, asked "Are you sure?" before the game closes. Quit Game on the pause menu opens a scroll with Return to Main Menu and Quit to Desktop, each taken at once; Return to Main Menu leaves for the title screen, a level left given up as Quit Level gives it up. The title menu is laid out by one rule, so a row added to it never leaves the screen or climbs over the logo, and every menu shrinks rows or a title that would run off the scroll or screen they are given.
- Mouse buttons 4 and 5 and the scroll wheel can be bound to actions. The keyboard's potion key can use the pad's gestures (hold to throw, double tap to shield) from a row of its own in the control settings, kept with the player's profile.
- Menus with more rows than fit scroll inside a window, by the wheel, the stick or the keys, with a bar and arrows at the column's right hand showing where the window stands; the bar is dragged and its arrows clicked. The wheel moves the window alone, and a pointer resting still chooses nothing as the rows pass under it. The control settings stand in one column with Apply and Restore Defaults below, a row toggled or captured leaving the window where it was, the players in Controls at full size in the middle of the screen; and no menu carries a Back row any more: the back control, Escape or the mouse's second button leaves a page.
- Old Thor, through the name code CYBMOS.
- The developer console opens on "CGaunt++" and the build, keeps half a space after its prompt, and completes the word being typed in dimmer ink; Right at the end of the line takes it. `goto` takes an installed Legends map (`goto <map>`, `goto <map> preview`, `goto n64|dreamcast <map>`, `goto back`) and `list maps` names them, in place of `legends_map` and `legends_play`. `party_count <1-4>` sets the level up for a party of that size and `party_count off` follows the players present again.
- Video offers the display the game is on when more than one is attached. A window moves to the display chosen, a fullscreen game keeps its mode there, and the choice is kept with the settings.
- The pointer steps aside while a pad is in use under an automatic control profile and returns with the mouse or keyboard.
- Developer console: `list switches` names a level's switches, `activate $SELECTED|switch#N|<id>|<name>` presses one as if the party stood on it, camera shot and all; `unlock` and `lock` open a door or chest without a key or shut it again; `list characters` names every playable character, locked and hidden too, and `unlock character <code>` or `lock character <code>` changes what the select screen offers. Switch pads, doors and chests can be clicked for `$SELECTED`.
- Online rooms choose their camera: Shared, the classic one camera for the whole party, or Solo, each player looking through their own single-player camera. The host's choice travels to every player and is kept for the next room hosted. Under solo cameras each player steers against their own view, nothing holds the party inside one picture, and whatever any player's camera sees counts as on screen: the enemies that wake and the generators that breed answer every camera, each generator judged against the one looking its way. A boss fight's camera and a scripted cut still show the same picture to all.
- Small arrows at the edge of the view point the way to each companion off this screen, in that character's colour, above the status boxes and gone the moment the companion is in sight.
- The developer console lets the view show through it while the pointer moves over it, and a click there picks what is beneath for `$SELECTED`; the pointer leaving the console brings its screen back.
- The netplay host page keeps the player limit and puts the game's rules on a Game Settings page of its own: difficulty, friendly fire, the camera rule and Aggressor Mode, which is a room rule now, starting from the host's own Game Options setting and set for everyone. Guests open the page to read the rules.
- Pickup counters stack over a player's status box, up to three at a time, centred, each icon's count updated in place while it shows; the stack rests just above the runestones and moves above the inventory label while that is up.
- Powerup names are spelt out in the English text ("Fire Breath", "Shrink Enemies", "Electric Shield", "Lightning Amulet"), and the ones that take a hyphen have one: X-Ray, Anti-Death, 3-Way Shot and 5-Way Shot. The menu font never had a hyphen; it has one now, cut from the crossbar of its own E.
- The game can draw through OpenGL 3.3 as well as Vulkan. Vulkan stays the default; a machine whose driver cannot start it plays through OpenGL by itself. The Video settings begin with a Renderer row, offered from the title screen only, that takes effect when the game is next started and says so; under OpenGL the depth of field, bloom and ambient occlusion rows are greyed, as that renderer does not offer scene post-processing.

## Fixed

- N64 destructible scenery accepts projectile hits even when the weapon meets the object immediately upon release.
- N64 ordinary enemies use their native models, animations, debris, and combat sounds instead of GameCube replacements.
- N64 generators retain their native damage stages but use half durability, rounded up per stage, while Dark Legacy player combat remains unchanged.
- N64 Skorne's projectiles aim at grounded players correctly, his roar uses its native sound, and grabbed players return to the arena instead of falling out of the map.
- N64 Skorne's arena starts with its intact floor cap, his entrance plays both native animation parts before his ready pose, and his roar particles appear during the stretch rather than afterward.
- N64 Skorne's ground punches emit their shockwave, damage, knockdown, sound, and camera shake when the fist reaches the arena, rather than during the wind-up behind him.
- N64 projectile impact effects retain their authored size instead of growing with the boss's model scale.
- All N64 music, sound effects, and dialogue use one source-aware loudness pipeline; quiet enemy sounds are raised without normalizing away authored volume, distance, or player settings.
- N64 music keeps its authored loop tempo while restarting the selected musical phrases correctly, preserving the repeated organ line in Altar of Skorne.
- The map and the stage preview on a level's loading screen are drawn as one picture each, joined from their four tiles and mended across the joins: where a row or column of tiles was cut to one side of its neighbours (the Mothership's preview has its lower half five texels off, which the original shows as a break in the runway) it is slid back into line, and the joins are softened, so no seam shows however the screen is scaled.
- The developer console lets the view show through it and picks beneath it in local play as well as online; the first round had wired only the online game.
- Level geometry is shaded as the original shades it: never by the level's light, a mesh with baked vertex colours showing them (at full strength, a five-bit 248 being the whole texture) under its lightmap, and anything else flat. Our renderer had read a flag the original never reads, so floors such as the Mothership's hangar, baked like their neighbours, were lit by the level's light instead and met them at a sharp seam (#74).
- A level no longer flashes the transition picture as it begins after its movie, or on any arrival: it cuts in the moment it has loaded, as the original does, the picture being only ever faded in on the way out of a level.
- The party starts a level facing the way the start marker points, into the level, and in the tower in from the door, as the original places it (#89); it had been turned half round.
- A movie's picture keeps to its sound: the movie's clock starts with its first frame of play rather than counting the time the level took to load, and then follows the sound as the mixer plays it, so a cutscene no longer drifts from its audio.
- Sumner announces a character that picks up Pojo by their own name, "<colour> <class> is now Pojo", where the original said "Pojo is now Pojo".
- The game no longer closes with "No mapping for the Unicode character exists in the target multi-byte code page" when its folder, its saves folder or the Windows user name holds a character outside the system code page. Files are opened by their full names, every path in a message or log is written as UTF-8, the settings folder and the command line come through whole, and on Windows 10 1903 and later the game runs with UTF-8 as its code page.
- Auto-Use Item keeps a Turbo pickup for later when the meter is already nine tenths full or more, instead of spending it.
- Red, silver and magic chests cycle their contents at the original thirty steps a second while they wait, so the X-ray's preview changes as it did.
- A strength potion's junk conversion shows inside chests.
- Platforms whose switches do not keep them solid wait under a player standing on them instead of moving on.
- Switching Aggressor Mode off during a level takes effect at once: the meter goes, generators breed by the level's own counts and the swarm thins back to its cap as the extras fall.
- The orange gems at the tower's foot keep their glow where a rune strip or tree stands before them: the parts of a pickup its flags sort behind composite after the scenery's own deferred queue, as the original's lowered sort key orders them.
- Sumner announces one gained level after another on the level panel; two players reaching it together no longer speak over each other.
- Continue on the stats panel keeps its white ink under the pointer, its glow held steady; the title and the panels mark text out through one painter.
- Typing a `goto` word no longer walks the disk for Legends maps every frame: they are looked for once, and again on `list maps` or `goto`. A word naming neither a level nor a map is answered as before.
- The Legends music sequencer is a build option (`GDL_LEGENDS_AUDIO`, on by default); off, nothing is fetched at configure time and a Legends map plays without its music.
- A gem's shadow no longer cuts a hole in the rune strip under it: a translucent sheet lying on the floor is a decal, drawn with the solids, so what writes depth over it blends over it as the original does.
- Aggressor Mode is off by default, and its grades step up at 63, 125, 188 and 250 kills rather than 25, 50, 75 and 100.
- Momentary bridges stay solid under a critter as well as a player or live enemy.
- The developer console's frame spike tracer (`spikes`, `GDL_SPIKE_MS`) has been removed.
