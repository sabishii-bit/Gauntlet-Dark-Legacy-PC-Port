# 1.0.0-beta

## Added

- Netplay diagnostics identify build compatibility mismatches, differing state hashes, rejected inputs, and resynchronization progress without logging join codes.
- N64 dialogue popups use their original font and scroll artwork through a reusable dialogue presentation interface.
- Gameplay mechanics can be added to the game as mods. Each mod in `src/mods` is built into the game, its mechanics appear as switches on the Game Options page, and an online host can turn each one on or off for the room. Aggressor Mode is now the first such mod: it lives on the `mods/aggressor` branch and is no longer part of the base game.

## Fixed

- Swings that damage a generator no longer count toward the run of hits the narrator praises, matching the original game.

- Removed the recurring FPS trace from the game log.
- Online resynchronization keeps the controls a player had already sent, so recovering from a desync no longer stops the game with a conflicting input history.
- A player being resynchronized is no longer judged by the state it reported before the repair, which could start a second resynchronization or stop the game.
- A desync is now repaired by resynchronization even when the host's own check is the last to arrive, or when a player dropping out leaves the others disagreeing; either used to end the online game.
- An online level resumed after a secret challenge can now be resynchronized.
- With solo cameras, a suicide bomber's cry no longer causes a false desync.
- Different field-of-view settings no longer cause a false desync while Sumner's hints are open.
- Toggling `noclip` from the console in an online game no longer desynchronizes the players when a late input rewinds the game across the toggle.
- A player who drops out of an online game now waits in the tower instead of holding up portals, pads and the shared camera. Travel, the shop, and skipping an entry movie or the level's opening camera carry on without them, packets they sent before dropping no longer end the host's game, and a drop during a resynchronization no longer leaves the others waiting.
- In three- and four-player games, everyone left holds a dropped player still from the same moment, even when a direct link brought that player's last controls to someone first.
- A player losing their connection while the host was still sending out the next stage no longer ends a four-player game for everyone else.
- When a player drops out in the shop, the others no longer risk leaving the game or ending it with mismatched purchases.
- A player whose game stops responding in an online game is now dropped by the host so the others carry on, whether it stalls during play, while the next stage loads, or while being resynchronized. The online game used to end for everyone. The notice that a player has stopped responding now also shows while the game is paused.
- A desync that resynchronization cannot cure now ends the online game instead of resynchronizing over and over.
- A minimized game window no longer holds the other online players at a level's entry or a completion movie.
- In the online shop, a player's game being held up for a moment no longer ends the online game, nor do the clicks, pointer movement or scrolling that pile up meanwhile, and catching up no longer plays every queued sound at once.
- A fallen player in an online game is no longer offered Quit Game, which ended the online game for everyone. Leave Game in the online menu is the way out.
- A player who drops out while reading one of Sumner's scrolls no longer leaves it open for everyone, and a dropped player's character waiting in the tower no longer sets off Sumner's visit.
- `goto` to a Legends map, back from one, or to a name that is not a level is now refused in an online game on every machine alike, instead of desynchronizing the players.
- Windows and Linux players no longer drift apart over collision surfaces or objects spawned from the console, and the desync check no longer mistakes how each platform stores its random-number state for a difference.
- A level resynchronized after a Stop Time pickup no longer leaves the players with different Stop Time totals.
- Dreamcast maps retain their native colored lightmaps and independent lightmap texture coordinates.
- Dreamcast torch sprites preserve additive blending, depth flags, and camera-facing orientation instead of drawing dark backgrounds.
- Sumner appears and animates in the Dreamcast tower at his native scripted position.
- Dreamcast gameplay cameras use native level ranges and camera markers, ignoring disabled bounds and attract-mode locators.
- N64 Ice grunts retain a visible body during their emergence animation instead of showing only their headlamps.
- N64 breakable walls flash white when struck by a nonlethal physical attack.
- N64 Death's health-drain arc loads and animates correctly, including its native color ramp and attachment offset.
- The N64 Plague Fiend's gas attack now follows its native animation root without an extra upward offset.
- Battle3's paired pressure switches reveal their bridge and keep it solid while occupied. The same fading-bridge behavior is supported across the other N64 maps using it.
- Tower2's cathedral light beam no longer hides the installed stained-glass shards.
- Tower2's cathedral departure floods the room with depth-based white fog and correctly travels to the Desecrated Temple. Hidden center pieces no longer cover its portal ring. The runestone circle displays its two-sided fire effect and both special routes suppress the ordinary transport sound and animation.
- N64 bosses animate while awaiting activation. Chimera's activation range no longer includes vertical separation from the player, and its breath effects follow the displayed head poses between animation frames.
- Chimera's simultaneous three-head breath uses the native combined-effect placement.
- N64 ranged auto-targeting prefers an eligible boss over nearby breakable pillars, while preserving scenery destruction and blocked-target fallback.
- N64 gameplay announcements use native Sumner health, item, and advice lines with their original color and class names. Unsupported characters, unavailable phrases, and missing or silent audio fall back to complete Dark Legacy announcements.
- Hell2's camera waits for Skorne's actual activation before tracking him, and native attack sound aliases restore missing cues. His ranged attack chains no longer attempt out-of-range grabs or claws.
- N64 Town and other Legends maps load generator models from their native enemy banks, restoring missing generators while retaining the Dark Legacy combat durability adjustment.
- N64 Death statues activate on contact or attack, and concealed Death releases from opened containers; a full enemy pool no longer loses the encounter. Sumner's native warning appears once per party when a player is level 10 or below.
- N64 legendary weapons are recognized and consumed in their corresponding boss fights, using native weapon models and sound cues with Dark Legacy combat mechanics.
- N64 progress survives returning to its tower and is included in autosaves. Quit Level returns N64 games to tower2 and Dreamcast games to levelt1. In N64 maps, `unlock_tower` unlocks N64 progression without changing Dark Legacy progression.
- Sumner's N64 History follows progression, tower gates and stained-glass shards restore from saved rewards, and reminders for missing runestones or obelisks continue across level transitions without repeating already collected rewards.
- Native world geometry respects its authored face-culling settings, fixing overlapping wooden fence faces in Town while retaining intentionally two-sided surfaces.
- N64 Town suicide bombers with green barrels release poison gas instead of a fire explosion.
- The N64 Plague Fiend's slime effects retain their native green color ramps and lighting, and completed tentacle animation tracks stop displaying a frozen mesh.
- N64 textures respect padded scanlines, correcting the Plague Fiend's scrambled healthbar artwork and other affected images.
- Native boss-completion narration plays when leaving the corresponding N64 stages and continues through the transition without restarting.
- Failed Legends map transitions preserve the current map and rewards, restore controls, and wait for fresh portal entry before retrying.
- N64 narration has a stronger shared loudness target, including the quieter Town entry announcements, while preserving user volume controls.
