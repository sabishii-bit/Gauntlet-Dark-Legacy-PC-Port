# 1.0.0-beta

## Added

- Dreamcast's six shipped boss arenas load their native models, move tables, effects, and collision nodes through the shared combat engine. Their bosses' attack distances and turning use the native fields; the Chimera rides its descending platform and waits for the authored activation trigger, including during level entry, and the boss camera turns to it as the platform starts down; and Skorne's entrance floor fragments respond to his authored animation cues. The arenas use their authored boss-camera records, the native boss attention point and world-derived limits, with a framing safeguard to keep players visible during focus transitions.
- Dreamcast boss victories play Sumner's native apparition, dialogue, voices, shard display, and Skorne runestone sequence before returning to the Dreamcast tower. Their rewards are granted once to living participants before the victory speech, without changing GameCube or N64 progression; players remain movable and protected from damage during the speech, and the camera keeps its position as it focuses on Sumner.
- Dreamcast activation pads drive their native linked world animations and moving collision, including hidden triggers and whole-party requirements.
- Dreamcast in-level dialogue uses native fonts and parchment through the shared menu controls.
- Dreamcast level entrances display their authored stage names with the original title animation.
- Dreamcast Sumner offers the original History, Obelisk Hint, Rune Hint, and Enemy Hint topics, selected from the party's separate Dreamcast progress.
- Dreamcast tower barriers, runestone displays, and stained-glass shards reflect the party's saved Dreamcast collections.
- Ordinary Dreamcast barrels and chests support damage, keys, native opening animations, and contained pickups. Random contents are selected when opened; unsupported scripted contents remain intact.
- Ordinary Dreamcast key-locked doors support collision, key use, and native opening animations.
- Dreamcast sound banks supply native transporter, chest, barrel, locked-door, and flame-contact sounds.
- Dreamcast standard generators and placed melee, archer, bomb-thrower, and suicide enemies use native models with shared combat behavior. Generators use their native breeding intervals and population limits while retaining Dark Legacy combat and difficulty settings, and show their active species' native models and textures through every damage state; the Desecrated Temple and Underworld generators spawn their native four-species broods, use the correct monster tiers, and support targeting and destruction. Enemies and generators route their combat sounds to the native banks, including hurt, death, melee, ranged attacks, and destruction.
- N64 Tower returns play the native runestone-placement, realm-door-opening, and shard-placement scripts at their native timing, one of each kind per visit, with timed captions and camera movement. The party stands facing Sumner as the visit opens, turns toward each piece he works on, and turns back to its arrival heading once the ceremony ends; Sumner turns to each piece and back to his podium, the cathedral beam fades in with the completed window, and his staff shrinks away afterwards.
- Netplay diagnostics identify build compatibility mismatches, differing state hashes, rejected inputs, and resynchronization progress without logging join codes.
- N64 dialogue popups use their original font and scroll artwork through a reusable dialogue presentation interface.
- N64 legendary weapons are recognized and consumed in their corresponding boss fights, using native weapon models and sound cues with Dark Legacy's combat mechanics and legend-item rite. In those fights the rite runs on through the boss's death throes, as Dark Legacy's does, but holds no one still, slows no one and lets potions go off. A boss whose legendary weapon the party brings wakes four seconds into the level wherever the party stands, as on the N64.
- Gameplay mechanics can be added to the game as mods. Each mod in `src/mods` is built into the game, its mechanics appear as switches on the Game Options page, and an online host can turn each one on or off for the room. Aggressor Mode is now the first such mod: it lives on the `mods/aggressor` branch and is no longer part of the base game.
- Dark Legacy's Sumner tells each character that has beaten Garm, once, that his old gateways have woken: a scroll the first time it comes back to the Tower with the N64 or Dreamcast campaign installed. Once Garm is beaten his menu also offers Legacy of Legends, a list of The Dark Legacy, Legend of What Once Was (N64), and Legend of What Could Have Been (Dreamcast), which the N64 and Dreamcast Towers' Sumners offer as well; a campaign that is not installed, and the Tower the party is already in, are greyed. Choosing a place sees the whole party off with the good wizard's teleport to that Tower, between any two of the three, with all it carries; Dark Legacy's Tower opens afresh at its entrance, where anyone who fell in a Legends campaign stands again, and with AutoSave on the party is saved each way. A Tower that fails to open leaves the party where it stood, and is greyed until the game is restarted. All of this is offline only.

## Fixed

- Dark Legacy's Throne of Skorne and Chimera arena lightning flashes at its native intervals instead of staying frozen, including during netplay and rewind.
- Dark Legacy bosses stay on the floor under them while asleep or frozen as well as while fighting, and settle where each step ends, so the Chimera rides its arena's lift instead of hanging in the air when the lift moves before it wakes, and the Spider Queen keeps to the slopes of her lair instead of trailing a step behind the ground.
- Dark Legacy's Chimera stays asleep until the whole party is in its arena and the lift starts lowering it, then wakes two seconds later, instead of waking as soon as the party approaches the arena; the boss camera turns to it, and powerups begin to run down, as the lift starts.
- Dark Legacy and N64 bosses count down their wake delays as the originals do, starting on the frame that arms them and checking from the next frame, instead of waking one frame sooner.
- Dark Legacy's Genie, Wraith and Spider Queen wake as soon as the party comes within range along the ground, as in the original, instead of waiting for it to come up to two and a half units closer because they stand above or below it, and no Dark Legacy or N64 boss wakes while its farthest player stands exactly on the edge of its range.
- Dark Legacy's Spider Queen starts inside her lair's territory, as in the original, instead of standing almost three units outside it until her first step snaps her back, and wakes for a party measured from where she stands.
- Dark Legacy's timed powerups keep running down three times as fast through a boss's death throes, as in the original, and stop only once it has finished dying, instead of stopping at the killing blow; they also start running down the moment a boss's fight begins rather than a frame later.
- Dark Legacy powerups keep working through the moment they run out, as in the original, instead of wearing off a frame early; the Dreamcast and N64 campaigns share the fix.
- Dark Legacy's legend-item rites run on through a boss's death throes, as in the original, instead of ending at the killing blow: a boss slain in its rite's dark before it roars keeps the level dark until it has finished dying, one slain mid-roar brings the light back as its death cuts the roar short, and a weakness that runs out meanwhile is still heard wearing off.
- Dark Legacy and N64 bosses keep to their territories by the original's measure of distance, whose edge lies up to a fortieth inside a circle at some headings, and Dark Legacy's Spider Queen and Lich judge how far they have strayed from home by where their last step would have taken them, as in the original, before they charge, grab or crack the ground.
